Visual Thinking Strategies Research

In my opinion, there are a few different types of images. The first type is when an article mentions something and includes a picture of it to help readers understand. The second type is an image placed near the title, which helps readers quickly understand what the article is about. Another type is the kind discussed in the New York Times article. These images focus more on creating a certain atmosphere. Instead of directly telling readers what they mean, they encourage people to observe and think about the meaning behind them. I think this is interesting because different people might understand the same image in different ways.

One website I found interesting is A24. The scrolling animations and the animated black frame that appears around the selected disc really caught my attention. When a disc moves to the center of the screen, its information gradually appears in the top left corner. I think this smooth transition makes the website feel really polished and professional. A similar effect also happens below the disc, where reviews from two well-known sources are displayed. I really like how the layout presents all this information without making the page feel crowded. Everything has enough space, but the important information is still easy to see. However, I think one drawback is that these animations might make it harder for people who just want to find information quickly. Overall, I think this website does a great job of combining visual design with interaction.

Overlays Design Pattern Research

Modal windows are something I encounter quite frequently while browsing the web. Sometimes they're pop-up ads; other times, they're login screens that appear when you first visit a website; and sometimes they're just notifications. I wouldnt' say I particularly dislike this design—perhaps I've simply gotten used to closing modal boxes without a second thought. However, the article's content made me realize that many people actually dislike this design, especially when it interrupts what theyr'e currently doing. This made me realize that when working on design projects, I cant' rely solely on my own experiences; I also need to gather others' perspectives on a design. Therefore, in my future designs, I'll only use modal boxes when absolutely necessary.

Research Form Design

This article introduces 20 practices that can help improve form design. Many of the small details mentioned in the article are things that I normally would not notice when using a product. However, after reading the article and thinking back to my experiences with different websites, I realized that these details can actually make a big difference in how easy a form is to use.

In a previous UI class, I designed a login interface myself. At the time, I did not consider many of the details mentioned in the article, especially the points about using examples and placeholder text. I used to like putting "e.g." inside text fields to show users what they should enter. However, after thinking about my own experience, I realized that this can sometimes be inconvenient. Once I start typing, the example disappears, so if I forget what format I am supposed to use, I have to delete what I entered just to see the example again.

Google is one of the websites I use most often, and I think its form design does many of these things well. Its interface is simple without leaving out important instructions.
